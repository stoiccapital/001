The principle

Both the body and a portfolio are compounding systems. They don't care about intensity, intelligence, or luck. They care about one simple action, repeated for a decade — add weight to the bar when the set gets easy, buy the index when the paycheck lands. Everything else — the supplements, the hot stocks, the macros, the trading strategies — is noise sold by industries whose business model is complexity.

The barrier isn't information. The information fits on an index card. The barrier is boredom tolerance — doing the same ordinary thing, week after week, while novelty-starved brains and marketing budgets tell you to do something different. A man who runs both systems for a decade lands in the top 0.01 %. Not because he's extraordinary, but because consistency on ordinary things is the rarest thing humans do.
